    Shortcut cheat sheet

    The idea

    Every decision comes down to two numbers: how often you need to win to make calling worth it, and how often you actually win. Compare them and the decision makes itself.

    FoldCallBet or raise
    0%what you needbet line100%

    Your chance of winning →

    What you need. Divide the pot by the amount you have to call. Call this the value. The bigger the value, the less often you need to win:

    Value (pot ÷ call)11.5234
    You need to win50%40%33%25%20%

    The bet line. To bet or raise you want to be a clear favourite. With more players in you have to beat all of them, so the line drops: 60% against one player, 47% against two, 40% against three.

    Before the flop

    Step 1 · Know your hand's rank. Every starting hand is ranked from 1 (best) to 169 (worst). Your tile shows your rank each hand, so you'll pick them up as you play. Find your two cards along the top and the side: same suit in the top-right half, different suits in the bottom-left, pairs on the diagonal.

    Step 2 · If nobody has raised. Count the players you still have to beat: everyone still to act, plus 2 for each player who just called the big blind (they've shown some interest). Then raise if

    rank × players to beat ≤ 120

    The more players left, the more likely one of them has a better hand, so the limit gets stricter. If your rank is over the limit, fold, or check if you're the big blind.

    Players to beat12345678
    Raise with rank up to12060403024201715

    Step 2 · If someone has raised. Start with the value (pot ÷ call), then take something off for each thing working against you:

    • Take off 1. At a value of 1 you'd need to win half the time, and almost no hand does that against a raiser.
    • Take off 1 for each caller. Their chips make the pot bigger, but now you have to beat them too.
    • Take off 0.1 for each player still to act. Any of them could re-raise and push you out.
    • Take off 1 more if it's a re-raise. Re-raisers hold much stronger hands.

    Multiply what's left by 80. That's your call limit:

    call if rank ≤ 80 × what's left  (never below 5)

    With a very strong hand, re-raise instead: rank 6 or better, rank 3 or better if someone has called, and only AA or KK against a re-raise.

    After the flop

    Step 1 · Find your hand on the ladder. Read across to the number of opponents still in. Each extra opponent is another hand you have to beat, so your chance drops quickly.

    Weak hands lose value as more cards come: ace high drops to 35% on the river, and nothing drops to 15% on the turn and 10% on the river.

    Step 2 · Adjust for how they played before the flop. If someone raised, they're likely strong: take off 10 (or 5 if other players are in too). If nobody showed any strength, add 10. Skip this with two pair or better or an overpair, which are strong either way.

    Step 3 · Add your draw. If you're one card away from a flush or a straight, add a bonus for the times you'll hit:

    DrawOn the flopOn the turn
    Flush or open-ended straight+30+15
    Inside straight+15+8

    Halve the bonus if you already have a pair or ace high, since you'd often win anyway. On the flop, the full bonus assumes you see both cards. If they can still bet the turn, use the turn number.

    Step 4 · Compare your total with what you need and the bet line, just like in "The idea".

    Bigger than you might expect, because loose players keep calling with worse hands, and you win more each time they do.

    About the numbers

    • Opponents are assumed to play sensibly: raisers hold their best 15% of hands, re-raisers their best 6%, players who call a raise their best 25%, and players who just call the big blind their best 50%.
    • The shortcuts were fitted to about 10,000 simulated decisions. Before the flop they match the simulation 98% of the time when nobody has raised and 89% when someone has. After the flop they match 86–88%.
    • Verdicts come from the simulation. Best matches it, OK is close, and Mistake loses money on average (counted in "EV lost").
    • Not included: bluffing, extra money won on later streets, acting first or last after the flop, and what later bets say about a hand.

      Counting cheat sheet

      The idea

      Blackjack is the one casino game where the odds move as the cards come out. Once a lot of low cards have gone, the shoe is full of tens and aces: you get more blackjacks (paid 3:2), your doubles land more often, and the dealer busts more. Counting tells you when that's happening, so you bet more then and the minimum the rest of the time.

      Step 1 · Keep the running count

      Start at 0 after every shuffle. Then for every card you see, yours, the other players' and the dealer's:

      Cards2 to 67, 8, 910 to A
      Count+10−1

      Cancel cards in pairs as they land: a 5 and a king together are 0, so you only track what's left over. The counting drill below trains this.

      Step 2 · Turn it into the true count

      A count of +6 means a lot more with 1 deck left than with 6. Divide by the decks still in the shoe (judge it by eye from the discard tray, to the nearest half deck) and round down:

      true count = running count ÷ decks left

      Step 3 · Bet with the count

      Bet 1 unit (the table minimum) until you're ahead, then add a unit for each point of true count:

      units = true count − 1  (at least 1, at most 6)

      The exact answer bets 1 unit for every 0.5% of edge, which keeps the swings to a size your bankroll can ride out.

      Step 4 · Play the chart

      Find your hand down the side and the dealer's card along the top. Check pairs first, then soft hands (an ace counting as 11), then hard totals.

      Step 5 · Change the play when the count says so

      A few hands flip once the true count is high or low enough. Learn them from the top down; the first few matter most.

      About the numbers

      • Every exact answer is worked out from the cards you haven't seen. The charts, count changes and bet table are worked out the same way for these rules, so they match how the coach grades you.
      • Checked over 3,000 simulated rounds for each rule set: the quick method matched the exact answer on 96–97% of plays (most of the rest were within 2% of the bet), 84–93% of bets and 93% of insurance calls.
      • For Las Vegas rules the count changes match the widely published ones (the "Illustrious 18") to within 1 point, and the house edge matches published figures.
      • Counting in your head isn't illegal, but casinos can stop you playing. Tables with a continuous shuffling machine can't be counted.
